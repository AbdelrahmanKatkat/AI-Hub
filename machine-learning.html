How to learn from this

Every lesson has the same rhythm: a practical question, intuition in plain words, a worked example on a tiny dataset, the formal algorithm, an animated lab you can edit, then traps, real uses, and self-check questions. Algorithm lessons also state what the model stores, what training and inference cost, and when to choose it.

The maths is linked, not repeated: when a lesson needs gradients, probability or matrices, it points to Mathematics for AI.

The pipeline every model lives in

Whatever the algorithm, the work happens in these six stages. Click a stage to see the computation and storage it needs. Each module of the course zooms into one or more of these stages.

Preview: watch a network learn

A taste of what the labs feel like. Pick a dataset, add layers or features, and press play: the coloured map is the model's decision, the lines are its weights, and clicking the map traces one point through the network. Module 6 builds this from first principles.

The map

Modules 0–1 set up the problem and the data. Modules 2–4 are the algorithm toolbox: linear models, classical methods, and ensembles — what wins most tabular problems. Module 5 is how you know a model is actually good. Modules 6–7 are neural networks and their architectures. Module 8 covers special settings (few labels, recommendations, time, uncertainty, causality). Module 9 is the system around the model: serving, monitoring, governance.
