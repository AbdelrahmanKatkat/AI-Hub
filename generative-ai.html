How to learn from this

Every lesson starts with a generation problem. Then comes a hand-worked example on a tiny vocabulary, a three-token attention head or a one-dimensional noise chain, the formal definition, an animated lab you can edit, traps, real uses and self-check questions. The labs are small toy models: real systems are far larger, so no claim is made about any particular commercial model.

The maths and ML are linked, not repeated: probability and information theory point to Mathematics, networks to Machine Learning, and image-specific generation to Computer Vision.

The map

Module 0 frames generative modelling: what it means to model a distribution and the families of methods. Module 1 builds language modelling, tokens and decoding, and Module 2 the attention mechanism and the Transformer. Module 3 trains large language models, and Module 4 aligns them with human preferences. Module 5 makes models cheaper and faster, and Module 6 builds prompting, retrieval and agents on top. Module 7 covers latent-variable and adversarial generators, and Module 8 diffusion and flow matching. Module 9 reaches multimodal models, evaluation and responsible use.
