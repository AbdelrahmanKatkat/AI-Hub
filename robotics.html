How to learn from this

Every lesson starts with a picture of a robot or a scene, then a hand-worked example on a 1–2 link arm, a grid, a one-dimensional filter or a small table, then the formal definition, an animated lab you can edit, traps, real robots and self-check questions. Each lesson says where it sits in the sense–think–act loop and how fast that loop must run.

The maths and ML are linked, not repeated: when a lesson needs matrices, Jacobians, Bayes or policy networks it points to the matching Mathematics or Machine Learning lesson.

The robot pipeline

Every robot runs these six stages in a loop, many times a second. Click a stage for what it does, what it needs and where its data is stored.

The map

Module 0 sets up frames and the software stack. Module 1 covers sensors and actuators, and Module 2 the kinematics and dynamics of arms and wheeled bases. Module 3 estimates the state of the robot and the map, and Module 4 perceives objects and scenes. Module 5 plans motion and tasks, and Module 6 controls it. Module 7 learns policies from demonstrations and experience, and Module 8 covers foundation models for robots. Module 9 makes it safe and keeps it running.
